Why Mend

Mend applies the same research-first approach I bring to every project to workplace conflict: the moment after a conflict with a coworker, when you need to figure out what to do next and don't have anyone unbiased to ask.

Mend is something I wish I had when I first started my career.

What I'm Trying to Accomplish

Taking Mend from a working prototype to a validated product, then a funded company.

PilotRun a 50–100 pair pilot cohort to prove employees will actually use it, and that it measurably cuts the time conflict currently costs a team.
Trust architectureGet the zero-trace, ephemeral-memory privacy model right first, it's the actual moat, not a feature list, and it's what makes the pilot results credible.
Legal reviewGet real employment counsel on the trigger-category definitions (the Title VII/harassment auto-escalation logic) before this goes near a real employee's real conflict.
Pricing validatedProve the below-enterprise-floor pricing model against one real signed deal, not just a spreadsheet model.
RunwayNon-dilutive credits and incubator support, Google for Startups among them, to fund that pilot before pricing equity against early traction.
