# Selling Mend to Managers

Why frontline managers are the easiest entry point, and how to reach them without an app store.

## Why managers are the easiest sell (vs. HR, vs. employees)

### Employees (hardest)
- Stigma cost: downloading a "conflict" app feels like admitting social deficiency.
- Wallet resistance: employees see workplace friction as the company's problem, won't pay $8-15/month out of pocket for it.
- Episodic churn: friction flares a few times a year, so subscriptions bleed during quiet months.

### HR / People Ops (middle)
- Big budget ($7,000-9,000+ ACV flat annual contracts) if ROI math works.
- Real pain: HRBPs lose 30-50% of their time acting as amateur counselors for non-statutory peer disputes.
- The catch: long sales cycles (30-90 days), high trust bar, and a security-vetting reflex. HR's instinct is control: "if I can't monitor it, how do I know it's not hiding problems?" Requires educating them that employees already hide conflict in backchannels, and that the Watcher's hardcoded Title VII safety floor protects compliance without giving HR visibility into routine disputes.

### Managers (easiest)
- Zero stigma: resolving team friction is literally the job description. Using a tool to diagnose why two reports are fighting looks like leadership, not an emotional crutch.
- Acute personal pain: most managers are "accidental managers," promoted for IC skill, not conflict mediation. They dread tough feedback and sprint deadlocks and want a private rehearsal space before the real conversation.
- Friction-free purchasing: managers often hold $200-500/year discretionary L&D or coaching budgets, or a team card, and can expense a small license without a procurement cycle.

## Go-to-market sequence: bottom-up manager wedge into HR

1. **Sell the wedge to managers.** Position as a private leadership flight simulator for high-stakes 1-on-1s and translating team friction into operational updates. Managers expense it under coaching/L&D budgets.
2. **Expand to employees via decoupled handoff.** When a manager generates a private, zero-trace link for two squabbling reports, those reports get the psychological safety of the tool without managerial snooping.
3. **Flip to HR.** Once several managers are already using it to deflect disputes, approach the Head of People: "Several of your managers already use Mend to deflect routine team friction. We can offer a company-wide license with SSO and our hardcoded Title VII compliance firewall, taking hours of refereeing off your HRBPs' calendars."

## How to actually reach managers (no app store)

Managers don't search an app store for "conflict resolution." Intercept them at the moment of acute dread, framed as a tactical tool, not therapy.

### 1. Intercept private venting in practitioner communities
Where managers go when something blows up: Reddit (r/ExperiencedDevs, r/techmanagers, r/ProductManagement, r/sysadmin), Blind, and Slack/Discord communities (Rands Leadership Slack, LeadDev, Product Collective, Indie Hackers).

Tactic: find a real venting post ("how do I deal with a senior engineer who won't review my junior's PRs?"), reply with a genuine, systems-level breakdown, and add a low-key footer: "I built a free zero-trace scratchpad that runs this diagnostic and scripts the 1-on-1, if you want to try it: [link]." Don't spam the link without the real answer first.

### 2. Zero-install web utility as the hook
Skip the app entirely. Ship a single-purpose web tool, no sign-in, no install:
- "The 1-on-1 Tough Talk Scaffolder" or "Slack Tone Buffer": paste a difficult message or raw frustration, get back a structural read and a non-defensive response in two sentences.
- Instant payoff with zero commitment and no corporate paper trail. If it works, they bookmark it or save it as a PWA.

### 3. LinkedIn content from the designer's POV
Dissect everyday broken tech rituals (PR review as psychologically hazardous ritual, fundamental attribution error in sprint planning, why forcing a 3-way meeting makes conflict worse). Tight 300-400 word posts with a diagram, written from a product/UX designer's seat, not HR-speak. This is the wedge that gets shared manager-to-manager.

### 4. Sponsor engineering/product newsletters
Target: The Pragmatic Engineer, Refactoring, Level Up, Product Growth, Wes Kao's newsletter. Hook stays tactical: "Tired of playing amateur referee on your squad? Free to try solo, expense it under your L&D stipend ($10/mo)."

### 5. Sell to the budget that already exists
Never ask a manager to pay out of pocket. Point at money they already have: "expense it under your annual Professional Development / Coaching budget" ($250-1,000/year at most tech companies). Give them the expense justification language directly in-app: "Expense Category: Manager Enablement / Executive Coaching Tooling."

## Checklist

- [ ] Find 5-10 live venting threads on Reddit/Blind/Slack communities and answer them for real, with the tool as a footer, not the pitch.
- [ ] Write 2-3 LinkedIn posts dissecting a specific broken tech ritual.
- [ ] Draft the one-line sponsor hook and shortlist newsletters to approach.
- [ ] Write the in-app expense-justification snippet for managers to copy into their own expense report.
