# Mend: Reaching HR Buyers (Go-to-Market Outreach)

Cold emails pitching generic HR tech fail: Chief People Officers and HR VPs get dozens of inbound pitches a day, mostly for benefits, payroll, or surveillance analytics. Pitching a tool that promises "management never sees the conversation" also triggers a defensive reaction from buyers whose job is risk management and organizational control — that objection has to be addressed head-on (see below), not hidden.

## 1. Reframe the Value Proposition for HR (the "relief" angle)

HR leaders don't buy software to "make people happier" — they buy software to eliminate administrative drain, reduce legal liability, and stop managerial burnout.

- **The problem HR actually hates:** HR Business Partners (HRBPs) spend 30-50% of their working hours acting as informal, amateur counselors for mid-level managers and squabbling coworkers over trivial friction (Slack tone, missed PR reviews, turf wars). It drains time away from strategic work.
- **The pitch pivot:** don't pitch Mend as "AI mediation." Pitch it as a **self-serve buffer for non-statutory friction** — it handles the petty, non-actionable friction in the "missing middle" that clogs HR's queue, while automatically escalating real compliance issues (Title VII, harassment, retaliation) straight to HR's desk via a fixed safety floor.

## 2. Bypass Cold Inboxes: High-Conversion Channels

### A. Private HR / people-leader communities

HR leaders rarely respond to cold vendor email, but they actively participate in invite-only practitioner networks (Charter, PeopleTech Partners, SafeSpace, Hacking HR, Resources for Humans/Lattice, CPOHQ) where they trade tool recommendations.

Never drop a product link directly — run a practitioner inquiry instead:

> "Quick question for HRBPs/People Ops: what percentage of your weekly 1:1 time is eaten up mediating everyday peer friction that never actually qualifies as a formal HR policy violation? Building research on whether autonomous, zero-trace dispute protocols can deflect that load — would love 10 minutes with two people who are exhausted by informal coworker drama."

### B. The "design partner" advisory approach

Executive buyers hate being pitched but like being treated as domain experts. Reach out to Heads of People at 80-200 person companies on LinkedIn:

> "Hi [Name], we've built an autonomous peer-resolution protocol designed to stop routine coworker disputes from escalating into formal HR cases. Before taking it to market, we need critical feedback from a seasoned People leader on our compliance safety floor (Title VII, ADA, harassment triggers). Not selling anything — would you be open to an 8-minute tear-down of our legal safety boundaries?"

This flatters expertise, signals the product takes workplace law seriously, and gets it in front of them without sales friction. Once they see it will clear their inbox of routine disputes, they tend to ask about a pilot on their own.

### C. Employment attorneys & fractional HR consultants

Target the advisors HR already pays for risk management:

- **Fractional CPOs / people-ops agencies** manage HR for 10-20 startups at once; one agency lead who likes the product can deploy it across their whole client portfolio.
- **Management-side employment attorneys** hate when trivial squabbles spiral into wrongful-termination or constructive-dismissal suits. Positioning Mend's zero-trace protocol and safety firewall to employment counsel builds credibility and turns them into a referral channel.

## 3. A Cold Outreach Script (under 90 words)

> **Subject:** deflecting routine peer disputes before HR / [Company Name]
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> Most HRBPs I talk to lose 4-6 hours a week acting as informal arbiters for peer friction that isn't an HR issue — it's just communication breakdown (PR delays, Slack tone, misaligned sprint priorities).
>
> We built Mend as a self-serve, confidential AI turn-taking tool for coworkers to resolve interpersonal friction privately.
>
> If it's a minor dispute, it resolves peer-to-peer with zero manager or HR footprint. If it touches Title VII, harassment, or retaliation, our fixed safety floor immediately routes it to your desk.
>
> Open to a 7-minute look at how we deflect routine friction away from People Ops?

## 4. Overcoming the "HR Control" Objection

The first fear any HR buyer will have: *"If employees use this in private, am I hiding toxic behavior I should know about?"* Address it in the first two minutes:

- **Acknowledge the reality.** Employees already avoid bringing everyday peer friction to HR for fear of being labeled "difficult." Today they vent to peers and build toxic backchannels instead. Mend doesn't hide conflict from HR — it resolves tension HR was never going to hear about anyway.
- **Highlight the immutable safety floor.** Hardcoded, non-configurable triggers halt the private room and hand the issue to HR the moment harassment, discrimination, bullying, or safety concerns are detected.
- **State the executive payoff.** Fewer petty complaints, faster sprint delivery, and a guaranteed escalation pipeline for true compliance risks.
