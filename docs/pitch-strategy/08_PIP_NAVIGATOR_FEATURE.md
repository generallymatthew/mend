# Mend: PIP Navigator Feature Concept

A feature for what to do when placed on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) — one of the most acute, isolating moments in a corporate career. Handled poorly, this looks like Mend is helping employees fight management, which would cause HR buyers to reject the platform outright. Handled correctly, it's a structured, objective calibration engine that protects both the employee's dignity and the company's operational clarity.

## The Strategic Dilemma

Mend is sold to HR leaders on reducing formal grievances, stopping manager burnout, and enforcing an immutable compliance floor. A PIP module cannot become an adversarial defense tool ("beat the PIP," draft legal pushback) — HR would see that as a Trojan horse. It has to be positioned as a **clarity & de-escalation protocol** that turns subjective, emotional friction into unambiguous, observable operational criteria for both sides.

## The 3-Phase PIP Navigator

### Phase 1 — Private intake & reset (de-biasing)

The employee typically enters angry and convinced the process is unfair. The AI acts as an objective mirror: validates the shock while stripping out emotional interpretation.

> "Receiving a PIP is destabilizing. Let's look at the document strictly as a contract. Paste the written objectives here, and we'll separate observable deliverables from subjective commentary."

Triage assessment, done privately:
- Is this PIP genuinely remediable with explicit metrics?
- Or is this an exit runway, where the priority should shift to negotiating a clean severance and preserving professional reputation?

### Phase 2 — Subjective-to-objective translation (gap analysis)

Most PIPs fail because goals are vague ("improve cross-functional communication," "show more senior leadership"). The tool converts ambiguity into specific, verifiable counter-proposals.

| Vague PIP item | Defensive reaction | Mend's reframe for the manager |
|---|---|---|
| "Needs to be more collaborative in sprint planning." | "I am collaborative! Mark just talks over everyone." | "I want to ensure I meet your exact expectations. Can we define this as: submitting written agenda items 24 hours prior and logging all architectural objections directly in the Jira ticket?" |
| "Code quality does not meet Senior level." | "My code is fine, they just nitpick formatting." | "Let's establish a concrete quality baseline: zero Sev-1/Sev-2 regressions during the 60-day window, and linters configured to enforce style automatically." |

### Phase 3 — Weekly check-in HUD & evidence ledger

A lightweight, private weekly tracker where the employee logs completed deliverables against PIP milestones. Includes a roleplay check-in: a 5-minute mock 1:1 with an AI "manager" before the real weekly review, to practice presenting completed work calmly without becoming defensive under pushback.

## Safety Floor & Compliance Rails for PIPs

- **No unlicensed legal advice.** The AI must never say "you have a wrongful termination case" or "this violates labor law." If an employee describes potential retaliation or discrimination (e.g. a PIP immediately following a pregnancy announcement or a reported accounting issue), the safety floor triggers an immediate referral to formal compliance or legal counsel — it does not attempt to adjudicate it.
- **No document-ingestion risk.** If an employee tries to upload proprietary code, internal financials, or NDA-protected records to "prove their performance," a client-side redactor scrubs sensitive data before it ever reaches model memory.

## Pitching This to HR Buyers

- **Eliminates bad-faith retaliation claims.** Employees who feel a PIP is arbitrary often respond with defensive, bad-faith HR grievances against their manager. Grounding them in operational reality reduces spurious escalations.
- **Solves the manager-training deficit.** Most first-time managers write vague PIPs that HR ends up rewriting by hand. Mend guides the employee to ask the right clarifying questions instead, saving HRBPs days of back-and-forth.
- **Clean outcomes either way.** Whether the employee recovers velocity and passes, or accepts the role isn't a fit and negotiates a dignified exit, the process moves forward quickly and calmly — without toxic team contagion or backchanneling.
