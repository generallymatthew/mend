Why there's no login — not laziness, the actual proof

It would be easy to build a login. We didn't skip it to save time; we left it out on purpose, because a login is exactly the thing that would let this promise be broken later.

The moment a company account exists, something somewhere has to store who's in it, who's using it, and what they said — even if nobody looks at it today, the capability to look is sitting there, waiting for a subpoena, a curious admin, or a policy change next year. A privacy policy can promise not to look. It can't prove there's nothing to find.

So instead of promising, we removed the thing that would need promising. There's no admin portal, no employee account, no database row anywhere that ties a name to a session — which means there is nothing we could hand over, nothing we could be compelled to produce, and nothing an employee has to simply trust us about. No login isn't a missing feature. It's how you can tell, from the outside, that there's no way for us to show anyone information about anyone.

What happens after you pay

  1. Stripe collects your company name, billing email, and payment — we never see or store your card details.
  2. You land on a short thank-you page. No login is created, because there isn't one.
  3. Within one business day, we email your billing contact a rotating team access code and a short rollout note to share internally.
  4. From then on, everything — invoices, seat refreshes, support, your monthly usage digest — comes by email. There's nothing else to log into, ever.
