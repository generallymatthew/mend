A note before you dive in

Thanks for testing Seam with me.

You're about to see an early, pre-legal-review prototype, not a finished product. A few minutes of reading this first will make your feedback more useful to me, and save you from guessing what you're looking at.

What you're about to see

Seam is the manager side of Mend: a private case manager for a people manager working through conflict between two of their direct reports (or a direct report and someone outside your team). You open a case, talk it through with Thimble as one ongoing chat, and it keeps a running action plan alongside the conversation instead of just logging a transcript.

This is a different tool from Mend's employee-facing side — here you're the manager, not one of the people in conflict, and the case persists across visits so you can pick it back up later.

Role-anonymized on purpose. What gets saved to the case is a stripped-down state (what the tension is, the last action, the next action) and the plan steps, never a transcript and never names. That's deliberate, not a gap.

How to go through it

  1. Take the short survey first, before you open a case. It asks how you currently handle conflict on your team, not how the tool just performed, so it needs to come before you've seen Seam color your answers.
  2. Open a case. Either describe a real (small, low-stakes) conflict you're actually managing between two people, or pick a scenario and roleplay it as the manager.
    Example scenarios
    • Two direct reports who disagree about who owns a shared process, and it's starting to show in how they talk to each other.
    • One of your reports is in an ongoing friction with someone on another team, and it's landing on your desk secondhand.
    • A conflict you'd normally handle by just telling both people to "work it out," except it hasn't worked.
  3. Talk to Thimble like you would a real tool. Interrupt it, correct it, push back on a suggested action step, go off-script. I'd rather you break it than be polite to it.
  4. Use the plan panel, not just the chat. Add, edit, or check off steps as the conversation produces them — that panel is as much the product as the conversation is.
  5. If it offers a peer session, try that path too. This test build only has one of you, so clicking it shows an explainer of what the real two-person session looks like instead of minting live links — that's expected, not broken.

What's intentionally missing

No voice here, text only. Just keep talking and using the plan panel until it feels like you've either finished a real case or run out of things to try, then close out with the short follow-up survey.

Two surveys, not one

One before you start, asking how you currently handle conflict on your team, on its own, before Seam has shaped your answer. One after, asking how this specific attempt went. Taking them in that order, not combined into one, is what makes the second one worth anything — it's measuring a change against your actual baseline, not just your general impression of the tool.
