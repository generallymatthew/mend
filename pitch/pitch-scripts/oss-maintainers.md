# Pitching Open Source Maintainers

Different shape of conflict than the core workplace use case: maintainers often don't know each other personally, there's no shared employer or HR to escalate to, and the disagreement is usually intellectual (a technical direction, API design, or governance call), not personal. What they lack isn't emotional intelligence about each other — it's a neutral, unbiased third party to drive the conversation to a decision.

This angle came from real tester feedback, flagged as a strong pitch fit — not yet used anywhere publicly, so test the framing before leaning on it hard.

## Opening line

> You've got six contributors stuck on one GitHub issue, nobody's being a jerk, but nobody's backing down either, and there's no shared manager or HR to force a decision. That's exactly the gap Mend's group-session mode is built for.

## Why it fits

- **No personal history to repair** — the goal isn't healing a relationship, it's reaching a group decision strangers can live with.
- **No authority to escalate to** — unlike a workplace, there's no manager who can just decide. Mend's facilitator (Thimble) is visible to everyone in the room, can't be muted or removed, and nudges toward a concrete proposal and a vote instead of letting discussion circle indefinitely.
- **Transparent by default** — this isn't the confidential 1:1 intake/private-session flow built for workplace peer conflict. Group sessions are open: everyone sees the same chat, proposals, and vote tallies. No side-conversation or safety-floor machinery here — that's scoped to the personal-conflict product line, not this one.

## The pitch

> Mend's group-session mode puts an always-present, neutral facilitator in the room — tagged clearly as "Facilitator," never a voting participant. It names the implicit proposal nobody's said out loud yet, asks the one question that actually moves the discussion, and nudges toward a vote when debate is circling rather than progressing. No one has to "win" the thread; the facilitator does the social labor of steering the conversation that a stranger wouldn't normally volunteer for.

## Recruiting this audience as testers

> We built an AI facilitator for exactly the situation your project probably hit this year: a real technical disagreement where nobody's being difficult, but six people with no shared history can't quite land on a decision. Would your maintainer team be open to trying it out on a real (or recent) stuck issue? We're looking for a handful of test cohorts before this goes further.

Keep this pitch scoped to the group/consensus use case specifically — don't blur it with the 1:1 confidential peer-mediation pitch in [hr-people-ops.md](hr-people-ops.md). They're different products with different trust models (anonymous/private vs. fully open).
