# Pitching HR & People Ops Buyers

Audience cares about: administrative relief, legal liability containment, and whether this is reliable software or fragile "AI slop." They do not buy software to "make people happier" — lead with risk and workload, not empathy.

Full field guide: [../docs/pitch-strategy/13_PITCHING_MEND_TO_ENTERPRISE_HR.md](../docs/pitch-strategy/13_PITCHING_MEND_TO_ENTERPRISE_HR.md). This file is the short version to use live in a conversation.

## Opening line

> Every HRBP I talk to spends 30-50% of their week acting as an informal referee for peer friction that isn't a policy violation — missed PR reviews, blunt Slack tone, sprint-priority turf wars. How much of your team's time goes to that?

## The pivot: don't sell "AI mediation"

Sell a **self-serve buffer for non-statutory friction** that automatically escalates the real issues:

> Mend handles the petty, non-actionable friction clogging your queue. Anything that touches Title VII, harassment, discrimination, or retaliation hits a hardcoded, non-configurable safety floor and routes straight to your desk — you get earlier visibility into real issues, not less.

## The three technical pivots (use when credibility is challenged)

- **"Chatbot" → deterministic state machine:** the LLM only does bounded translation tasks at controlled intervals; an independent Watcher audits every turn before it's shown; the system halts programmatically if anything steps outside procedure.
- **"Trust me" → hardware-level ephemerality:** sessions run in volatile memory enclaves. There's no durable database of employee chat logs to subpoena, leak, or breach.
- **"Hiding issues" → the immutable safety floor:** Mend doesn't hide dysfunction from HR — it resolves friction that was never going to reach HR anyway, while guaranteeing real compliance issues surface faster than an annual survey would catch them.

## The "HR control" objection

First fear: *"If employees use this privately, am I missing something I should know about?"*

> Employees already avoid bringing routine friction to HR for fear of being labeled difficult — today they vent to peers or build backchannels instead. Mend doesn't hide anything from you that was ever going to reach you. The safety floor is the one thing that isn't private: harassment, discrimination, bullying, and safety concerns halt the session and escalate immediately.

## The ask: a 30-day design partner pilot, not a contract

> We're not asking for an enterprise-wide contract today. We want a forward-thinking People leader to stress-test our compliance floor with one 15-25 person team for 30 days. If it doesn't deflect hours from your managers and resolve friction cleanly, you turn it off, no commitment.

## Closing math

> Preventing one regrettable departure on a single team saves $45,000-$60,000 in replacement cost. At $750/month for the whole org, Mend pays for itself if it resolves one serious misalignment every six months.
