## Current beats (as drafted)

1. Hi, I'm Matthew. I live in Durham, NC, and I've spent 15 years as a UX designer
2. I have 3 kids, and one day while I was playing around with Claude, they came down to discuss a very important issue they were having: who stole whose charger. And if you've ever listened to these stories, you know they're both boring and confusing
3. I wished I could have them explain it to an AI who'd have the patience to last through the whole story. I thought it'd be nice if they could just sit around an AI and try to convince it who was right
4. But I realized it's not just my kids who could use this kind of help
5. And it brought me back to my first job in UX: [hunger games story goes here]
6. Data backs up this need
7. We spend years honing our craft but are expected to swim the moment we're thrown into the deep end
8. What I could have really used was a confidant who could help me navigate a complicated situation
9. Because I had no good options, friends told me I needed to show leadership by being strong
10. I couldn't go to my manager, and I knew HR was a risky move to reach out to for help
11. This is why I'm building Mend: to help people negotiate social dynamics in the workplace, in a safe, confidential way that has the worker's best interest at heart and is willing to tell them the truth, not just what they want to hear
12. While my kids are still arguing about chargers, here's a screenshot of the actual advice Thimble gave for what I would have gotten back then
13. Here's a screenshot of what my manager at the time would have been advised, if he'd asked, on how to choose a temp replacement
14. I know what I'm building, and I finally figured out who's going to pay for it and why
15. I need more people to test out the application
16. Meet more HR professionals to understand what considerations they'd have when thinking about the need for something like this

- Beats 12 and 13 are two screenshots of real Thimble answers, not live demos. Still two separate examples in one slot, worth checking they don't eat time meant for the close.
- Beats 15 and 16 are the close and they're good: concrete, specific asks. Keep these, don't dilute with more asks.
