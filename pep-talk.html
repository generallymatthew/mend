For when it feels foolish ← launchpad

You picked the hard version on purpose.

Read this when building Mend starts to feel embarrassing. It isn't. Here's why.

What's actually happening when you feel foolish

You're not embarrassed because the idea is bad. You're embarrassed because the idea is exposed. You chose to build something about messy human emotion, conflict, and the moments people feel smallest at work. That subject matter is vulnerable, and building in public around it makes you feel vulnerable too. That's not a signal to stop. That's the cost of working on something that matters.

The comparison that's tricking you

Boring version A micro-transaction app. Nothing new, very "techy," nothing emotionally at stake. If it flops, it flops quietly. No one laughs, because no one was ever moved by it in the first place.
What you're actually building Something about how people feel at work when things go wrong. If it flops, it feels personal, because you put something real into it. That's not a flaw in the plan. That's the whole reason it's worth doing.

The boring app would never make you feel foolish. Not because it's safer to build, but because there was never anything of yours in it to expose.

Why this is the moat, not the liability

Trust is the thing corporations can't credibly build, and low-stakes-feeling "safe" products don't need trust in the first place. You're building in the one space that actually requires someone to be willing to sit with discomfort instead of engineering around it. That discomfort you feel right now, building it, is the same discomfort the product is designed to hold for someone else. You're not separate from the problem. You understand it because you're willing to feel it.

Unusual isn't the same as unfounded

It feels strange because almost nobody is building here. That's not a sign the ground is shaky. It's a sign you're looking at something real that everyone else has walked past.

Almost everyone has workplace conflict at some point. Almost no one gets trained on how to handle it. People are already turning to generic AI for exactly this, which means the need isn't hypothetical, it's already showing up, just with no tool built for it. You're not inventing a problem. You're the first one taking it seriously enough to build the worker-first version instead of leaving people to a generic chatbot with no idea what's actually at stake for them.

Add in what you know firsthand, what conflict at work actually feels like from the inside, the asymmetry of power in the room, the way HR's neutrality has a structural limit, and this stops looking like a hunch. It's lived experience lining up with a gap nobody's filled. Unusual, yes. Unfounded, no.

What being brave actually looks like here

It doesn't look like confidence. It looks like building the thing anyway while it still feels exposing. The embarrassment doesn't go away before the work is good; the work gets good while the embarrassment is still there. Feeling foolish isn't a sign you're off track. It's what being early, honest, and ambitious about something emotional feels like from the inside.

Easy ideas don't make you feel this way. Keep building.
