Thimble expressiveness lab

Four ideas from the animation/empathy research pass, each wired onto the real avatar engine so they can be felt, not just described. Toggle one at a time against the baseline, or run a full simulated turn with everything on.

idle
1. Anticipation

A ~160ms inward "gather" the instant you stop talking -- before the state label even flips to Thinking. Telegraphs that Thimble noticed, rather than just catching up a beat later.

Disney's anticipation principle: a wind-up before the action reads as intentional. Right now Thimble is purely reactive -- this adds the missing beat.

2. Backchannel ack

A quick "mhm" double-nod on a pause, separate from the existing loud-input ear-ripple. Click You pause (ack) above while Listening to fire it on demand.

HCI research on voice-UI feedback: synchronized micro-motion (not a static icon) is what raises perceived "it's really listening," distinct from just reacting to volume.

3. Squash & stretch on landing

A one-shot squash/rebound the instant any new state lands, on top of the existing color/shape easing. Gives the landing a sense of weight instead of just sliding into place.

Squash & stretch is the Disney principle most tied to "tactility" in flat UI -- it's what separates a lively object from one that just glides.

4. Energy mirroring

Idle/listening breathing and nod pace track a simulated "your energy" level instead of a fixed cadence -- higher energy speeds the cycle up, lower energy slows it down.

VR-agent empathy research: behavioral mimicry (echoing the human's own pace) raised perceived empathy more than verbal attunement alone.

5. Listening signal

The actual gap behind "listening doesn't read as listening": this lab's Listening state had nothing simulating your voice, so the wobble just sat flat. Now a fake speech-shaped level drives it, plus a soft glow pulse -- not the old polygon ear-ripple -- fires on a loud moment. Click Listening and wait a beat.

Voice-UI feedback research: it's the synchronized reaction to YOUR voice, not a static "Listening" label, that reads as "it's really paying attention."
