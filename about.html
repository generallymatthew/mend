The Mend Manifesto

We value mending relationships over:

  • Structural clarity over litigating blame. Workplace friction stems from colliding incentives, competing KPIs, and overloaded schedules, not malice or "bad people." We solve the system, not the personality.
  • Radical privacy over managerial surveillance. Honest resolution cannot survive an audience. People only own their part when their words cannot be weaponized on a performance review or an HR docket.
  • Personal agency over forced corporate harmony. We never demand apologies, preach toxic positivity, or force coworkers to become friends. Setting clear, functional operational boundaries is just as valid.
  • Decoupled consent over performative truces. Concessions made under live social pressure fall apart the moment people leave the room. True agreements are evaluated and signed alone, with zero coercion and no audience.
  • Dissolving sutures over permanent dossiers. The process exists purely to stabilize the dynamic while it repairs. Once alignment holds, the record dissolves completely.

What We Mean by “Solving the System”

When an argument feels personal, the instinct is to diagnose the other person as difficult, incompetent, or toxic. But in high-velocity teams, conflict almost always comes down to structural design:

  • Colliding Incentives. Engineering is penalized for regressions and system instability, while Product is measured on delivery speed. Both are doing their jobs, which puts their day-to-day workflows on a direct collision course.
  • Unclear Swimlanes & SLAs. Vague ownership, ambiguous handoffs, and misaligned turnaround expectations create friction that masquerades as interpersonal hostility.
  • Operational Fixes. Instead of trying to fix personalities, we fix the machine, aligning delivery criteria, defining explicit handoff boundaries, and removing structural mismatches so both people can succeed without friction.
