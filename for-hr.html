Why this is your problem already

Three reasons this belongs on your radar, not your workers' alone.

01
Your employees are already doing this, just not safely
People are pasting real names, real incidents, and real grievances into consumer AI chatbots to get advice on a work conflict — with no policy covering it, no data agreement, and nothing stopping that text from being retained, trained on, or surfaced later. Banning it doesn't stop it; it just moves it further from your sight. Thimble gives them a purpose-built, zero-trace place to do the same thing, so the sensitive data isn't sitting in a general-purpose chatbot's logs.
02
It gives managers a sounding board, so fewer things land on your desk
Most managers have never been trained to handle conflict on their team, so the default is to escalate to HR earlier than necessary, or avoid it until it's worse. A manager who can think a situation through first, privately, is a manager who handles more of it themselves — competently, and without needing you in the room.
03
Conflict resolved peer-to-peer holds up better than conflict HR resolves for people
The research and the practice both point the same way: resolution that people reach themselves, without a manager or HR adjudicating it, is more durable and leaves less residue than a resolution imposed on them. Thimble is built to help people get there on their own, which means fewer open cases, not a new one for every conversation it touches.
