# Getting Warm Intros: Alumni, Mentors, Scouts, Judges

Goal here isn't a sale. It's a recommendation into an incubator's internal partner-review pipeline, which moves faster than a cold application. Flatter their expertise, ask for feedback, not for a favor — the referral follows naturally if they like what they see.

## Who to target

- **Recent alumni founders** (1-3 years out) of the specific program you want into. Ask for a short product-feedback chat, not a reference.
- **Venture scouts / active angels** who source deals for a program.
- **Mentors, judges, legal/design advisors** the program already uses — met at local tech events, university clinics, or pitch nights.

## The outreach message (under 100 words)

> Subject: quick question on de-escalation tech / [Incubator Name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I follow your work with [specific portfolio company or sector, e.g. future-of-work / enterprise AI].
>
> I've built an autonomous, confidential peer-resolution protocol that defuses workplace friction before it reaches HR, running entirely in volatile memory with zero persistent transcripts. We have [X] pilots running and are refining the safety-floor architecture now.
>
> Would you be open to a 5-minute take on how you'd evaluate adoption friction for zero-retention enterprise tooling?
>
> [Name]
> [Link to live prototype or short demo]

## After the chat

If they respond well, ask directly:

> This was genuinely useful feedback. If it's a fit, would you be open to flagging it to [program]'s partners? No pressure either way.

Don't ask for the recommendation up front — it reads as transactional and most people won't vouch for something they haven't actually looked at.

## What makes this work

A working link beats a deck. Send the live prototype, not screenshots — see [../docs/pitch-strategy/05_USER_TESTING_AND_COHORT_STRATEGY.md](../docs/pitch-strategy/05_USER_TESTING_AND_COHORT_STRATEGY.md) for what "ready to show" looks like at this stage.
